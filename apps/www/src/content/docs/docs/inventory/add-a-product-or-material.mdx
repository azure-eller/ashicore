Create the item everything else references. Pick whether it is a material or a product, choose the unit you stock it in, then fill in buying, costing, pricing, and traceability details.

<p class="po-meta">
  <span class="po-chip">5 steps / ~5 min</span>
  <span class="po-chip">type is permanent</span>
  <span class="po-chip">autosaves</span>
</p>

<Aside type="caution" title="Treat unit changes carefully">
The app treats **item type** as a creation-time decision. The item-card UI can change the family stock unit and mirror it to variants, but existing inventory history stays denominated in the unit used when those events were posted.
</Aside>

<Steps>

1. **Pick the type, name, and stocking unit.** From **Inventory**, choose **New Material** or **New Product**. Enter the name, optional SKU and category, and the **Unit** used for stock quantities and valuation. The new card remains unsaved until both required fields are present; its save indicator says **Name is required** or **Choose a unit to save**, and the missing name is highlighted.

   <Frame title="<b>New material</b>">
     <div class="mk-pad">
       <div class="mk-rows mk-rows--3">
         <Field label="Name" required focus>Screened Compost</Field>
         <Field label="SKU">CMP-SCR</Field>
         <Field label="Unit" required>yd3</Field>
       </div>
     </div>
   </Frame>

2. **Set how you buy it.** If you buy in a different unit than you stock, set **Purchase unit** and **Conversion factor**. The factor is stock units per purchase unit: a truckload with factor `18` becomes 18 stock units.

   <Frame title="<b>Supply details</b>">
     <div class="mk-pad">
       <div class="mk-rows mk-rows--3">
         <Field label="Purchase unit">Truckload</Field>
         <Field label="Conversion factor">18</Field>
         <Field label="Default purchase price">$420.00</Field>
       </div>
     </div>
   </Frame>

   <Aside type="note" title="Purchase unit rules">
   Leave purchase unit blank when you buy and stock in the same unit. If you set one, it must differ from the stocking unit and needs a positive conversion factor. See [Purchase vs. stock units](/docs/purchasing/purchase-vs-stock-units).
   </Aside>

3. **Set how you sell it.** Turn on **Sellable** if the item can appear on sales orders. If customers order it differently than you stock it, select **Use a different sales unit**, choose the **Default sales unit of measure**, and review the **Unit conversion rate**.

   <Frame title="<b>General information</b>">
     <div class="mk-pad">
       <div class="mk-rows mk-rows--2">
         <Field label="Sellable">Yes</Field>
         <Field label="Sales unit">Use a different sales unit</Field>
         <Field label="Default sales unit of measure">Pallet</Field>
         <Field label="Unit conversion rate">1 sales unit = 3 1 Yard Totes</Field>
       </div>
     </div>
   </Frame>

   Ashicore calculates compatible conversions. Enter the factor yourself for a real packaging conversion it cannot infer, such as the number of cups in a 50 lb bag. Sales orders and prices use the sales unit; inventory and recipes stay in the stocking unit. See [Sales vs. stock units](/docs/sales/sales-vs-stock-units).

4. **Set stock, cost, price, and traceability.** Add safety stock, default prices, current stock unit cost, barcodes, supplier item code, lead time, and minimum order quantity as needed. **Default selling price** is per sales unit when one is configured. Use **Lot tracking** on the item family to choose individual lots or the untracked shared bucket. Switching the mode needs inventory-admin access and is blocked while open orders, stocktakes, or blocked stock reference the item.

   <Frame title="<b>Cost &amp; stock</b>">
     <div class="mk-pad">
       <div class="mk-rows mk-rows--3">
         <Field label="Current stock unit cost">$23.333333</Field>
         <Field label="Default selling price">$31.00</Field>
         <Field label="Lot tracking">Tracked</Field>
       </div>
     </div>
   </Frame>

   <Aside type="note" title="Opening stock posts inventory history">
   The create form accepts an initial **stock** quantity. Later stock edits are treated as adjustments, not silent quantity rewrites. Use [Adjust stock](/docs/inventory/adjust-stock) when you are correcting physical inventory.
   </Aside>

5. **For products, add manufacturing details and a recipe.** Products can carry a manufacturing mode, batch yield fields, operation costs, and BOM rows. Batch mode requires an expected batch yield. A valid recipe records components, lot-age constraints, and operation costs as BOM revision history. The Recipe tab shows each ingredient's current estimated cost contribution per finished unit; manufacturing orders can swap a BOM component to another active variant in the same item family.

   <Frame title="<b>Premium Potting Mix</b> / Recipe">
     <Grid>
      <thead><tr><th>Component</th><th class="num">Quantity</th><th class="num">Cost</th><th>Requirements</th></tr></thead>
       <tbody>
         <tr><td>Screened Compost</td><td class="num">0.5 yd3</td><td class="num">22.50000 USD</td><td>None</td></tr>
         <tr><td>Coir Pith</td><td class="num">0.3 yd3</td><td class="num">9.60000 USD</td><td>30-day minimum age</td></tr>
       </tbody>
     </Grid>
   </Frame>

</Steps>
