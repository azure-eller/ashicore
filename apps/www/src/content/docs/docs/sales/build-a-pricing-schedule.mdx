Pricing schedules suggest prices per sales unit for product sales-order lines. A schedule combines customer scope, product item scope, and quantity breaks measured in each matched line's sales unit.

<p class="po-meta">
  <span class="po-chip">4 steps · ~3 min</span>
  <span class="po-chip">route: <b>/sales/pricing</b></span>
  <span class="po-chip">quantity breaks</span>
  <span class="po-chip">lowest matching price wins</span>
</p>

<Steps>

1. **Set the customer scope.** From **Sales > Pricing**, create a new schedule. Name it, then choose whether the schedule applies to a customer category or all customers.

   <Frame title="Ashicore · <b>Pricing schedules</b>">
     <span slot="action"><Button variant="primary">New Schedule</Button></span>
     <Grid>
       <thead><tr><th>Schedule</th><th>Customers</th><th>Items</th><th>Breaks</th></tr></thead>
       <tbody>
         <tr><td>Wholesale nursery</td><td>Wholesale</td><td>Bulk soils</td><td>3</td></tr>
         <tr><td>Spring promo</td><td>All customers</td><td>Selected items</td><td>2</td></tr>
       </tbody>
     </Grid>
   </Frame>

   <Frame title="<b>Wholesale nursery</b>">
     <div class="mk-pad">
       <div class="mk-rows mk-rows--2">
         <Field label="Name" required focus>Wholesale nursery</Field>
         <Field label="Customer scope">Customer category</Field>
       </div>
       <Field label="Category">Wholesale</Field>
     </div>
   </Frame>

   | Customer scope | Meaning |
   | --- | --- |
   | Customer category | The schedule applies to customers in that category. |
   | Blank category | The schedule applies to all customers. |

   A customer in that category can match both this schedule and an all-customers one — the line takes whichever yields the lower price.

2. **Choose the item scope.** Choose which sellable products the schedule can cover. Exact duplicate scopes are blocked, but broader and narrower schedules can still overlap.

   <Frame title="<b>Item scope</b>">
     <div class="mk-pad">
       <div class="mk-rows mk-rows--2">
         <Field label="Scope">Category</Field>
         <Field label="Item category">Bulk soils</Field>
       </div>
       <Field label="Preview" muted>Premium Potting Mix, Raised Bed Blend, Garden Blend</Field>
     </div>
   </Frame>

   | Item scope | Required details |
   | --- | --- |
   | All | All sellable products. |
   | Category | Product category. |
   | Variant | Product variant option and variant value. |
   | Selected | One or more selected products. |

   You can't create two schedules with the exact same customer-and-item scope; where scopes overlap, the lowest resulting price wins.

3. **Build quantity breaks.** Add at least one break. Breaks use the matched item's sales unit: a break at 10 means 10 pallets for a Pallet item, not the stocking quantity inside those pallets. Breaks must stay in ascending order and cannot overlap. An open-ended break must be the final row.

   <Frame title="<b>Quantity breaks</b>">
     <span slot="action"><Button variant="subtle">+ Add break</Button></span>
     <Grid>
       <thead><tr><th class="num">Min qty</th><th class="num">Max qty</th><th class="num">Discount</th><th>Label</th></tr></thead>
       <tbody>
         <tr><td class="num">1</td><td class="num">9</td><td class="num">5%</td><td>1-9 yd3</td></tr>
         <tr><td class="num">10</td><td class="num">24</td><td class="num">10%</td><td>10-24 yd3</td></tr>
         <tr><td class="num">25</td><td class="num">—</td><td class="num">15%</td><td>25+ yd3</td></tr>
       </tbody>
     </Grid>
   </Frame>

   | Field | Rule |
   | --- | --- |
   | Minimum quantity | Required and greater than 0, in the matched item's sales unit. |
   | Maximum quantity | Optional, in the same sales unit. When blank, the break is open-ended. |
   | Discount percent | Required, from 0 through 100. |

4. **Save and test on an order.** Add a covered item to a sales order for a covered customer. The line pricing lookup snapshots the winning schedule name and break label, then fills the suggested unit price.

   <Frame title="<b>SO-2077</b> · line pricing">
     <Grid>
       <thead><tr><th>Line</th><th>Customer</th><th>Schedule</th><th class="num">List</th><th class="num">Unit</th></tr></thead>
       <tbody>
         <tr><td>Premium Potting Mix · 18 yd3</td><td>Wholesale</td><td>Wholesale nursery · 10-24 yd3</td><td class="num">$38.00</td><td class="num">$34.20</td></tr>
       </tbody>
     </Grid>
     <div class="mk-pad mk-pad--top">
       <Totals rows={[
         { label: "List subtotal", value: "$684.00" },
         { label: "Schedule discount", value: "-$68.40" },
         { label: "Line total", value: "$615.60", grand: true },
       ]} />
     </div>
   </Frame>

   If the line uses base price instead, check that:

   | Check | Why |
   | --- | --- |
   | Customer category | The customer must match the category scope, unless the schedule applies to all customers. |
   | Item scope | The item must fall within the schedule's item scope. |
   | Quantity | The ordered sales quantity must fit one break. |
   | Item type | Pricing schedules apply to products; sellable materials use their base price. |

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip" title="How overlapping schedules resolve">
Ashicore does not treat the narrowest schedule as automatically stronger. It collects every schedule that matches the customer, item, and quantity, then uses the lowest resulting unit price.
</Aside>
